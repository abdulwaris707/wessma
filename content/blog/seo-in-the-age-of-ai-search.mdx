Search has changed more in the past two years than in the previous ten. Users ask longer questions, AI overviews answer many of them directly, and answer engines cite a handful of sources instead of listing ten blue links. The question we hear most: *is SEO still worth it?*

The short answer is yes — but the playbook has shifted.

## What still works

- **Technical foundations.** Fast, crawlable, well-structured sites with clean schema are still the price of entry. AI systems read the web too.
- **Genuine expertise.** First-hand experience, original data and clear authorship are what get cited.
- **Topical depth.** Owning a topic with a connected cluster of pages beats publishing scattered articles.

## What changed

Traffic for simple informational queries has dropped, because the answer now appears on the results page. Meanwhile, **commercial and comparison queries** — where people are close to a decision — are more valuable than ever.

We now prioritise:

1. Bottom-of-funnel pages: comparisons, pricing, alternatives and use cases
2. Original research and data that others will reference
3. Structured content that answer engines can extract cleanly

## Measuring what matters

Rankings alone are no longer the scoreboard. We report on organic pipeline and revenue, branded search growth and citations in AI answers. If your SEO report does not connect to revenue, it is time for a new report.

## A practical checklist

- Audit Core Web Vitals and fix anything in the red
- Add Organization, Product, FAQ and Article schema
- Publish one piece of original data every quarter
- Build comparison and alternatives pages for your category
- Show authors, credentials and real experience on every article
