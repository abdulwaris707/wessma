AI has moved from boardroom buzzword to line item. But most businesses we speak to are stuck at the same place: a few impressive pilots, nothing in production. The difference between a demo and a return on investment is **choosing boring, high-volume work first**.

## 1. Instant lead qualification

When a lead fills in a form, an AI assistant replies within seconds on email or WhatsApp, asks two or three qualifying questions and books a call directly in your calendar. One of our real-estate clients cut average response time from four hours to four minutes — and doubled qualified leads.

## 2. Support triage and drafting

An agent reads every incoming ticket, tags it, pulls the customer's history and drafts a reply for a human to approve. Support teams typically handle 30–40% more tickets without adding headcount.

## 3. Document extraction

Invoices, purchase orders, contracts and application forms are read, validated and pushed into your ERP or CRM. What used to take a person a full day now takes minutes, with a human reviewing only the exceptions.

## 4. Sales research briefs

Before every sales call, an agent compiles a one-page brief: company news, funding, tech stack, decision makers and relevant case studies. Reps walk into every call prepared.

## 5. Weekly reporting

Pull data from ads, analytics, CRM and finance tools, and generate a plain-English weekly summary with the three things leadership should know. No more Monday-morning spreadsheet marathons.

## How to pick your first automation

Score every candidate on three criteria:

- **Volume** — how often does this task happen?
- **Time** — how long does it take a person?
- **Risk** — what happens if the AI gets it wrong?

Start with high volume, high time and low risk. Add human review wherever the risk is higher. Measure a baseline before you build, so you can prove the ROI afterwards.
