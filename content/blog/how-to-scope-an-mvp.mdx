Every founder we meet has the same instinct: build everything, then launch. It feels safer. It is actually the riskiest thing you can do. After shipping more than 60 MVPs, we have learned that **speed comes from focus, not from cutting corners**.

## Start with the one workflow that proves your business

Before we open Figma, we ask a single question: *what is the one thing a user must be able to do for this business to exist?* For a marketplace, it is a successful transaction. For a SaaS tool, it is the moment a user gets their first result.

Everything in the first release exists to support that workflow. Everything else goes on a "later" list — and we keep that list visible, so nobody feels their idea was thrown away.

## The three-bucket scoping exercise

We run every feature through three buckets:

- **Must have** — without it, the core workflow breaks.
- **Should have** — makes the workflow noticeably better, but users can live without it for a month.
- **Could have** — nice ideas that do not change whether the product works.

A healthy MVP is roughly 60% must-haves, 30% should-haves and 10% polish. If your must-have list is longer than your should-have list, you have not been ruthless enough.

## Do not cut the boring parts

The things founders want to skip — authentication, roles, billing, error states, analytics — are exactly the things that make an MVP feel real. We build them properly from the first sprint because rewriting them later is far more expensive.

> An MVP should be minimal in scope, never minimal in quality.

## Plan for the week after launch

The biggest mistake is treating launch as the finish line. Before we write code, we agree on:

1. The three metrics that define success in the first 30 days
2. How we will collect qualitative feedback from the first 50 users
3. Which "should have" features are next in line

## The 10-week shape

| Weeks | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Discovery, scope and success metrics |
| 2–3 | UX flows, UI design and clickable prototype |
| 4–9 | Development in two-week sprints with weekly demos |
| 10 | QA, launch and analytics |
